{{if eq .Grid.Kind "tag"}}

A tag marks a property of a document that the YAML specification leaves to the implementation, or settles beyond the grammar: a key written twice, an alias to an anchor of another document, a tab before a node. The corpus vocabulary holds {{len .Head.Corpus.Vocabulary}} tags. Each names the stage where the question arises (parse, compose or construct), and the answer the specification requires when it settles one. Each parser's stance file declares its stand on the tags it has ruled on. Hover over a tag to see its stage and rule.

{{else}}

A feature is a construct the document contains, as the generator wrote it: a flow collection, a CRLF break, an anchor, a "!!" shorthand. Features decide nothing about the verdict or the value: they show which constructs the differences gather on.

{{end}}

Each cell counts, for one parser, the cases on the row that the parser reads otherwise than the corpus with no reason in its stance file, or with only an open tag: first the verdict, then the value. Differences that a settled rule, a declared stand or a departure explains are not counted here; the reasons tables of the summary count them. Click a count to list its cases.

The split column counts the cases on which the parsers give different verdicts or build different values, explained or not. The higher the split, the more the parsers disagree on this corner of the specification.
